Suicide awareness & prevention

This page won't describe methods or details, it will tell you what to watch for, what to say, and what actually helps.

This year I lost a friend to suicide. Writing this page is one of the hardest and most necessary things on this whole site. If you're reading it because you've lost someone too, I'm sorry for your loss.

Warning signs

Not everyone shows obvious signs, but many people do, often in ways that are easy to miss or explain away:

  • Talking about being a burden, feeling trapped, or having no reason to live
  • Withdrawing from friends, family, or activities they used to care about
  • Giving away possessions, or tying up loose ends without a clear reason
  • A sudden calm or improvement in mood after a period of deep depression
  • Increased use of alcohol or other substances
  • Saying goodbye in ways that feel more final than usual
  • Changes in sleeping or eating patterns, or a loss of interest in the future
You don't need to be certain You don't have to be sure someone is suicidal to check in with them. A wrong guess costs you an honest conversation. A right guess you miss can cost a life.

If you're worried about someone

Ask directly

"Are you thinking about suicide?" It feels like a frightening thing to say out loud. It does not put the idea in their head, research consistently shows the opposite. Most people are relieved someone finally asked plainly.

Listen without trying to fix it immediately

You don't need the right words. You need to actually hear them, without rushing to cheer them up, argue them out of it, or change the subject because it's uncomfortable for you.

Don't promise secrecy

It's tempting to say "I won't tell anyone" to earn trust. Don't. Tell them instead that you care too much to keep this to yourself, and help them find real support.

Help them get to real support

Offer to sit with them while they call a helpline, or go with them to a clinic. Don't leave someone in crisis alone if you can help it. See Get Help for numbers and places in Botswana.

Follow up

One conversation isn't the end of it. Check in again the next day, and the week after. People in crisis often remember who kept showing up.

If the thoughts are yours

If you've been having these thoughts yourself: you are not weak, dramatic, or a burden for feeling this way. It's a sign that something in you needs real support right now, the same way a fever is a sign your body needs help. Please reach out to someone; a friend, a family member, or one of the services on the Get Help page. You don't have to explain it perfectly. You just have to reach.

After a loss

If you've lost someone to suicide, the grief that follows is its own particular weight; guilt, anger, unanswered questions, sometimes all at once. None of that means you failed them. Grief like this deserves support too, not just silence and "moving on."
