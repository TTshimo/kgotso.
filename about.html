Why this exists

I wrote my way out, so I write for the people still finding their way

I'm Tumisang. I started writing as a teenager, not because I set out to become an author, but because it was the only way I knew how to survive losing someone I loved wholeheartedly. I survived a loss no kid should have to know. Writing was where I put what I didn't have words for anywhere else, and without really planning it, that grief shaped everything I wrote afterward. My work has been centered around mental health ever since.

Information is knowledge, and knowledge is what shapes us; how we understand ourselves, and how we understand each other.

Somewhere along the way I realised that if my books were reaching people who recognised themselves in the pages, the least I could do was hand them something more useful than a story to close and put down; a way to actually understand what they were feeling, and somewhere real to take it.

Why a QR code and not just a chapter

Fiction can get close to a feeling in a way that's hard to match. But a story can't hand you a phone number, or tell you the warning signs to watch for in a friend, or explain what a toxic home does to a person years later. This site is the part of the book that isn't a story; it's the practical, plain-language follow-up for whoever needed to read that page.

This year

I find myself in a familiar place again. I lost another friend to suicide this year, and once more I've turned to the same two things I know how to do with a loss this size; writing, and raising awareness. It's part of why the suicide awareness page on this site exists, and part of why this whole project feels less optional to me now than it did when it was just an idea. If you've lost someone too, you're not carrying something unusual, you're carrying something enormous, and it deserves real support, same as anything else on this site.

If you want to reach out, share a story, or tell me a resource is missing or outdated, this site will always have a way to contact me.
