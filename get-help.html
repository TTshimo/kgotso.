Life-threatening emergency right now?

Call 997 for ambulance or 999 for police, toll-free from any Botswana network. Stay with the person if you safely can.

Real places

Get help in Botswana

Most mental health resources online are built for the US or UK and useless from here. These are numbers and services you can actually reach.

Talk to someone now

Lifeline Botswana / FTMTB

7552 7590  ·  7380 7492

Free, confidential counselling for anyone in distress, including suicidal crisis. Reachable on either number, or from outside Botswana at +267 7552 7590.

Botswana Network for Mental Health

0800 600 961 (toll-free)

Support for anyone struggling with their mental health, or worried about someone who is.

Childline Botswana

116 (toll-free)

Free, confidential counselling and support for children and young people. Note operating hours can vary — if there's no answer, try the numbers above.

Botswana Gender-Based Violence Helpline (BGBVC)

Free tele-counselling and support for anyone affected by gender-based violence, with in-person offices in Gaborone, Francistown, and Ghanzi.

Reading from outside Botswana?

These are free, well-established online directories that connect you to a helpline or crisis service wherever you actually are.

Find A Helpline

findahelpline.com

Search by country to get free, confidential helplines and crisis lines near you; phone, text, and chat options, filtered to your location.

Befrienders Worldwide

befrienders.org

A global network of emotional support centres focused on suicide prevention. Their site has a country directory to find your nearest member centre.

International Association for Suicide Prevention (IASP)

iasp.info/resources/Crisis_Centres

A directory of crisis centres worldwide, maintained by the leading global suicide-prevention organisation.

Child Helpline International

childhelplineinternational.org

For children and young people specifically, helplines in over 140 countries for abuse, bullying, and mental health concerns.

If you're in immediate, life-threatening danger, contact your local emergency services number first, these directories are best for ongoing support and non-immediate crisis, not a 911/999-style dispatch.

Ongoing, in-person support

  • Your nearest government clinic or hospital, ask for the mental health or psychiatric unit; primary healthcare facilities across Botswana are increasingly equipped to refer or support directly.
  • Princess Marina Hospital, has psychiatric services and can refer you onward.
  • A private counsellor or psychologist, if that's accessible to you, search for registered practitioners in Gaborone, Francistown, or Maun.
  • Faith or community leaders, for many people in Botswana, a trusted pastor, elder, or community figure is a real and valid first step, alongside professional care.
Numbers change Helplines occasionally shift or go quiet. If one of these doesn't connect, try another on this list, or go to your nearest clinic in person; someone there can point you further.

How to make the call easier

  • You don't need the right words. "I don't know how to start, but I'm not okay" is enough.
  • You can call on someone else's behalf if you're worried about a friend.
  • If speaking feels too hard right now, writing it down first, even just a few words, can help you get started.
